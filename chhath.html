Thousands of Bihari sons and daughters working far from home want to stand at their own ghat this Chhath. Flight fares have tripled. We are raising funds so that no son, no daughter has to tell their mother, "Is saal nahi aa paayenge."

Why we are asking

Chhath is not a holiday. It is a homecoming.

For a Bihari, Chhath is the one week in the year when the whole family stands together, knee-deep in cold water, waiting for the sun. Nahay Khay, Kharna, Sandhya Arghya, Usha Arghya. Four days that a mother keeps fasting for, without even a drop of water on the last night, while she waits for her children to come home.

This year, many of our brothers and sisters working in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai and Delhi are trying to go home. Trains were waitlisted months ago. And the only way left, the flight to Patna, Darbhanga or Gaya, now costs more than a month's salary for a security guard, a delivery partner, a housekeeping worker, a junior cook.

"माई फोन पर पूछती है, कब आ रहे हो? मैं कहता हूँ, देख रहा हूँ टिकट। सच बोलने की हिम्मत नहीं होती।" Ramesh, 27, works at a warehouse in Bengaluru. Home is a village near Chhapra.

We are a group of friends from Bihar living in these cities. We have made a list of people who have asked for help. We check each one. We book the ticket in their name directly, so the money goes only to the fare. Nothing else.

What they are going home for

चार दिन, जो साल भर की थकान धो देते हैं

नहाय-खाय

Day 1. A dip in the river, and the house is scrubbed clean. Kaddu-bhaat is cooked on a mud stove.

खरना

Day 2. Gur ki kheer and rotis, eaten once at dusk. After this, the vrati will not touch water for 36 hours.

संध्या अर्घ्य

Day 3. The soop filled with thekua, sugarcane, bananas and coconut, lifted to the setting sun.

उषा अर्घ्य

Day 4. Before sunrise, the family stands in the water again. When the sun comes up, the fast ends. Everyone is home.
