PARV 23 OF 30 · KARTIK

Chhath Mahaparv

छठ महापर्व

Kartik Shukla Chaturthi to Saptami (October–November)

"उगऽ हे सूरज देव, भइल अरघ के बेर"

Katha · The story

Why we keep it

The vrat is older than any of its stories. The Mahabharat says Kunti worshipped the sun and was given Karna; that Draupadi kept it in exile so the Pandavs could regain their kingdom. The Ramayan says Sita kept it on the banks of the Ganga at Munger when she and Ram returned to Ayodhya. The Puranas tell of Priyavrat and queen Malini whose dead son was revived by Shashthi Devi. Older still is the plain sun worship of the Rigveda, which Chhath keeps unchanged: no priest, no idol, no temple, the vrati standing in water speaking directly to the sun, at its setting and at its rising.

For Malinagar, Chhath is the festival that brings everyone home. The ghat on the Budhi Gandak is cleaned by the young men a week before, the path lit with diyas and the bamboo arches. Nahay Khay, Kharna, Sandhya Arghya, Usha Arghya: four days in which the vrati, usually the mother, fasts without water for thirty-six hours while her children, from wherever they are, stand with her in the river. The Chhath Ghar Wapsi Fund on this site exists for the ones who cannot afford the ticket.

Mahatva · Significance

What it means

Chhath is the identity festival of Bihar. It thanks Surya for life and harvest and asks Chhathi Maiya for the children's health. It is the only festival where the setting sun is honoured first, a lesson in gratitude for what has passed. Its food is cooked at home from the harvest, its soop is bamboo, its prasad is shared with every stranger at the ghat; it is a festival that cannot be bought.

"काँच ही बाँस के बहँगिया, बहँगी लचकत जाए"
A line everyone at the festival knows

Vidhi · How it is done in Malinagar

Step by step

  1. Day 1, Nahay Khay (Chaturthi): river bath, the house purified, one meal of kaddu-bhaat and chana dal cooked on a mud chulha.
  2. Day 2, Kharna (Panchami): day-long fast broken at dusk with rasiao kheer and roti after offering to the moon. The nirjala vrat begins.
  3. Day 3, Sandhya Arghya (Shashthi): thekua and kasar made in the afternoon; the soop filled with thekua, sugarcane, banana, coconut, singhara, radish, turmeric plant and the lit diya; the procession to the ghat; arghya of water and milk to the setting sun.
  4. Day 4, Usha Arghya (Saptami): to the ghat before dawn; arghya to the rising sun; the vrati breaks the fast with prasad and the family shares the soop with everyone present.

Bhojan · What is eaten

Thekua, kasar, rasiao kheer, kaddu-bhaat, whole fruits with stalks, sugarcane, and the prasad shared at the ghat.

Yaad rakhiye · Good to know
