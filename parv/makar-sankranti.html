PARV 27 OF 30 · MAGH

Makar Sankranti (Tila Sakrat)

मकर संक्रांति · तिला सकरात

14 January, when the sun enters Makar

"दही-चूड़ा, तिलकुट, और माघ के धूप"

Katha · The story

Why we keep it

On this day the sun turns north and the days begin to lengthen: Uttarayan. Bhishma, lying on his bed of arrows, waited for this day to die, because a death in Uttarayan goes straight to heaven. The elders say it is the day the gods wake after their six-month night. In Bihar it is named for its food, til, the sesame that keeps the body warm in the Magh fog: tilkut, tilwa, lai, and the gur that binds them.

The village day is dahi-chura: the new rice beaten into chura, eaten with fresh curd and gur before anything else is touched, then tilkut, then the kites go up over the wheat fields and stay up until the evening khichdi.

Mahatva · Significance

What it means

It is the farmer's winter festival, the turn of the sun, and the end of the fog's worst. In Mithila it is Tila Sakrat; in the rest of Bihar Khichdi, after the evening meal. The daan of til and khichdi to the poor is the day's duty.

"तिल के लड्डू, गुड़ के मिठास, माघ के पहिल दिन"
A line everyone at the festival knows

Vidhi · How it is done in Malinagar

Step by step

  1. Dawn bath in the river for those who can bear it; others bathe at home with til in the water.
  2. Dahi-chura with gur and tilkut eaten first, the elders feeding the children.
  3. Daan of til, rice, dal and a blanket to a Brahman or a poor family.
  4. Kites all afternoon; khichdi with ghee, chokha, papad and achar at night.

Bhojan · What is eaten

Dahi-chura, tilkut, lai, tilwa, and the evening khichdi with aloo-baingan chokha.

Yaad rakhiye · Good to know

The tilkut of Gaya is famous, but the tilkut of Pusa chowk is what a Malinagar child remembers.
