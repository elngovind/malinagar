बाईस टोले
The twenty-two tolas of Malinagar

"रास्ता पूछो तो कोई दिशा नहीं बताता, टोला बताता है।"

Malinagar is one village and its own gram panchayat, but nobody lives in "Malinagar". Everyone lives in a tola: a hamlet with its own chowk, its own pokhar, its own Holika fire and its own way of saying the village's name. This page is the list, with the five every child can point to and seventeen waiting for you to name.

Gaon ek nazar mein

The village in numbers

From the 2011 Census, the last full count. The real number today is higher at Chhath and lower the rest of the year.

9,074people (4,689 men, 4,385 women)
1,725households
22tolas, by the village's own count
876 haarea, of which 623 ha is sown
62.6%literacy (men 71.8%, women 52.9%)
26 kmto Samastipur; 13 km to Kalyanpur block HQ

Malinagar is its own gram panchayat with an elected mukhiya, and has a post office, a bank branch, an anganwadi and a health sub-centre in the village. Nearest villages: Saidpur and Sahuri to the west, Bakhtiarpur and Hajpurwa to the south-east, Madanpur to the east, Nankar to the north-west, Pusa Deopur across the river to the south-west.

Poori soochi

All twenty-two

Five are filled in from the village blog. The dashed cards are the ones we have never managed to count in one sitting. Send the names, the direction and one line about each, and they go in.

01ThahraथहराCentre

The middle of the village: the chowk, the bus point and the way to every other tola. Directions in Malinagar are given from here.

02Pakka Tolaपक्का टोलाSouth, toward the river

The tola nearest the Budhi Gandak and the ghat; the Chhath procession passes through it. The name says the houses were pucca before the rest.

03DhobgamaधोबगामाEast

Also written Dhruvgama on the old blog. The eastern tola, on the way to Madanpur.

04Badgamaबड़गामाNorth

The 'big village' tola on the north road toward Nankar and Sembhu Ram Tok.

05SimriसिमरीWest

The western tola, toward Saidpur and Sahuri, and the Pusa–Kalyanpur road.

Har tola mein

What every tola has

Chowk

The crossing where the tola meets: the tea stall, the barber, the Holika fire in Phagun and the Saraswati pandal in Magh.

Pokhar

The pond: for the cattle, the Bat Savitri banyan, the Sama Chakeva clay and the Durga visarjan when the river is too far.

Gachhi

The mango orchard the tola sleeps in during Jeth, and where the Teej jhula is hung.

Sthaan

The tola's own shrine: a Brahm Baba, a Dih Baba or a Sokha under a tree, where the first diya goes before the family's own door.

Khalihan

The threshing ground shared by the tola's farmers, busiest in Baisakh and Aghan.

Boring

The shared tube-well and pumpset, and the argument about whose turn it is for water.
